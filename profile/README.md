## 🎯 Welcome to the OSU Public GitHub Organization

Welcome! This is the official public GitHub organization for Oregon State University. It serves as a **"safe haven"** for OSU faculty, staff, and researchers to publish open-source software, configurations, and documentation that have been approved for public release.

Our mission is to foster a culture of open collaboration, transparency, and innovation. By providing a centralized and managed platform, we aim to showcase the incredible work being done at our university while ensuring compliance with university policies and open-source best practices.

## 🤔 What is the "Safe Haven" Concept?

The "safe haven" model is designed to simplify the process of publishing open-source work. Once your project is approved, you can transfer it to this organization, and our automated systems will handle the rest. This automation ensures that every project meets university standards for public release by:

-   **Adding a Standard License:** Automatically applies an approved open-source license (e.g., MIT, Apache 2.0) to your repository.
-   **Applying File Headers:** Inserts the appropriate OSU copyright and license headers into your source code files.
-   **Including Community Health Files:** Adds standard `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, and security policy files.

This process reduces the burden on individual developers and guarantees a consistent, professional, and legally sound presentation for all OSU public projects.

## 🚀 For OSU Employees: How to Publish Your Work

Ready to share your project with the world? The process is straightforward.

### Step 1: Get Approval
**All code, configurations, and documentation must be approved for public release *before* being published here.** This step ensures that no sensitive, confidential, or proprietary information is accidentally exposed.

➡️ **Start the approval process by [visiting the OSU Technology Transfer Office (TTO) portal](https://research.oregonstate.edu/tto/disclose-your-innovation) or contacting [your designated IT support group].** (Please replace this link with the correct internal URL).

### Step 2: Prepare Your Repository
While our automation handles the legal boilerplate, a great project starts with you! Before submitting for transfer, please ensure your repository includes:

-   A clear and descriptive `README.md` file explaining what your project does, how to install it, and how to use it.
-   Clean, well-commented code.
-   Any necessary documentation or examples.

### Step 3: Transfer to the Organization
Once you have received approval, you will be invited to transfer your repository to the `oregonstate` organization. Our technical team will guide you through this simple process.

### Step 4: Automation and Publication
After the transfer is complete, our automated workflows will run. They will open a Pull Request in your repository with the added license, file headers, and community files. Once you review and merge this Pull Request, your project will be fully compliant and publicly visible under the Oregon State University banner.

## 📖 What Can Be Published Here?

This organization is home to a wide variety of projects, including but not limited to:

-   **Software Code:** Research software, utility scripts, web applications, and libraries.
-   **Infrastructure as Code:** Terraform, Ansible, Docker, or other configuration files.
-   **Documentation:** Technical guides, standards, and project documentation.
-   **Data Schemas & Models:** Non-sensitive schemas and models that support university systems or research.

**Important:** This platform is **NOT** for storing or publishing sensitive data, including FERPA, HIPAA, PII (Personally Identifiable Information), or CUI (Controlled Unclassified Information).
